What happens at the Hub

The Hub’s peers run the room, the welcome and the Tuesday meal. Visiting organizations bring a foot care nurse, legal advice, health care, recovery support, identification and housing help on their own schedules. Call 403-606-7892 to check what is on before you travel.

Meals

Three a week in the building, about 800 plates.

The Hub’s peer kitchen team cooks on Tuesdays and a partner organization cooks on Thursdays. A separate Saturday meal runs from 2:00 to 5:00 p.m. Clothing and hygiene supplies when available.

Peer support

Run by the Hub.

Nine peers with lived experience are paid to work the room on every service day. Talk, or just sit. Both are fine.

Help with ID, income and housing

Visiting organizations.

Help with identification, income support and housing, offered on each organization’s own schedule. A peer can tell you what to bring.

Indigenous wellness

Indigenous communities carry their own cultural programming, ceremony, and gathering. COH provides the space where that work happens.

Health care

Visiting organizations.

Wound care, community nursing and testing for sexually transmitted and blood-borne infections are offered at the Hub on varying days. You can walk in. Call 403-606-7892 to ask when a service is next available.

Foot care nurse

Visiting nurse, on some service days.

A foot care nurse sees people at the Hub. You can walk in. Call 403-606-7892 to ask when the nurse is next in.

Legal help

Visiting legal organization, on some service days.

Legal advice in the same room, from a visiting legal organization. Call 403-606-7892 to ask when it is next on.

Indigenous Peer Support Workers

Training created by and for Indigenous people.

Indigenous Peer Support Workers are trained through a program created by and for Indigenous people with lived and living experience of homelessness, mental health challenges, substance use and involvement in systems. Rooted in Indigenous ways of knowing, teachings and values, it blends classroom learning with hands-on practice at the Hub, where peers offer one-to-one support, cultural connection and help navigating services. Three cohorts and 23 participants so far, aligned with Peer Support Canada standards.

Housing stability support

In development.

The Hub is starting peer support for people moving into housing, alongside the housing worker who holds their file. Results will be shared once the first group has finished.

What the Hub is

Central Outreach Hub is a day space of about 12,300 square feet in the lower level of Central United Church, at 131 7th Avenue SW. On each service day the door count averages about 290 visits: people come in to eat, get warm, rest and talk with peers who have lived through homelessness and recovery themselves.

The Hub is a program of Central United Church, a registered charity (118844042RR0001). It is led by Program Director Brad Pert and Creative and Community Director Tamara Llewellyn, with nine paid peers and a team of volunteers on the floor. The church receives and administers all funds and issues tax receipts.

For many people the Hub is a gathering space where they already have relationships and a sense of belonging. Other organizations work from the same room, each running and answering for its own service, so an introduction to a nurse, a lawyer, a recovery worker or a housing worker can happen on the spot, when the person is ready.

About four in ten visits are by Indigenous people. Indigenous communities lead their own cultural work in the building, and the church makes room for it.

How the room works

Three things make the room work.

Walk-in access

One room where anyone can come in during open hours. Entry does not require an appointment, a referral or ID. Some visiting services have their own requirements, and a peer can tell you what to bring.

Visiting organizations

Organizations offer their own services in the Hub on their own schedules. COH schedules the space and sets shared expectations for everyone working in the room.

Peers on every shift

Experienced peers work each shift with the Hub’s leads, welcome people and introduce them to visiting workers when they want that.

Activity to date

The Hub is open two days every week, year-round. From February 3 to September 1, 2026, about 17,600 visits were recorded at the door across 60 service dates, an average of about 290 visits per service day. Someone who leaves and comes back is counted each time, and the total includes repeat entries and one estimated count.

Volunteers give an estimated 60 hours on each service day, based on counts from ten service days in February and March 2026.

These figures count visits and hours. They do not yet show what changed for people.
